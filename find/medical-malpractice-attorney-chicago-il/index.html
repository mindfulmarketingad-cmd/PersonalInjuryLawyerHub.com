What a medical malpractice attorney handles

Medical malpractice claims allege that a healthcare provider's care fell below the accepted professional standard and caused harm. These are among the most procedurally demanding personal injury cases, and many states impose requirements that do not apply to ordinary negligence claims.

Common medical malpractice attorney cases in Chicago, Illinois

  • Misdiagnosis or significantly delayed diagnosis
  • Surgical errors, including wrong-site surgery and retained instruments
  • Medication and dosage errors
  • Birth injuries to mother or infant
  • Failure to obtain informed consent, and failure to monitor a patient's condition

How these claims are proven

A bad outcome alone is not malpractice; medicine carries risk even when care is appropriate. The claim requires showing what a reasonably competent provider would have done in the same circumstances and how the actual care departed from it, which effectively always requires testimony from a qualified medical expert. Many states additionally require a certificate or affidavit of merit filed at or before the complaint, impose separate and sometimes shorter limitation periods, and cap non-economic damages. These requirements vary widely, so confirming them locally and early is essential.

Compensation in medical malpractice attorney claims

Claims can include the cost of corrective treatment, ongoing and future care, lost income and earning capacity, and non-economic damages, subject to any applicable state caps.

Deadlines that apply

Every state sets a statute of limitations for injury lawsuits, and claims involving a government entity often carry far shorter notice deadlines measured in months. Because those periods differ by state and by claim type, confirm the deadline that applies to your situation with a lawyer licensed in Illinois rather than relying on a general figure. What to expect when you contact a firm

Most medical malpractice attorneys offer a free initial consultation, and the great majority work on a contingency fee, meaning you owe no attorney fee unless they recover money for you. That first conversation is usually a screening call: they will ask what happened, when it happened, whether you have received medical treatment, and whether you have spoken with any insurer.

If the firm takes the case, the early work typically involves sending a letter of representation so adjusters contact them instead of you, gathering the police or incident report, collecting your medical records and bills, and identifying every insurance policy that might apply. They will generally wait to value the claim until your treatment stabilizes, because settling before then means settling without knowing what your future care will cost.

Before signing anything, ask who will handle your file day to day, what the fee percentage is and when it increases, whether case costs are deducted before or after the fee is calculated, and what happens to those costs if the case is unsuccessful. Get the fee agreement in writing and read it away from the office.

Frequently asked questions

How many medical malpractice attorneys are listed in Chicago, Illinois?

Our directory currently lists 17 firms serving Chicago, Illinois. You can compare them on the map above by rating, review volume, and business type.

How much does it cost to hire a medical malpractice attorney in Chicago, Illinois?

Most personal injury firms work on contingency, meaning you pay attorney fees only if they recover money for you, and initial consultations are typically free. Percentages and the treatment of case costs vary between firms, so ask for the fee agreement in writing before signing.

Do I need a lawyer for a medical malpractice claim?

Not every claim requires representation. Minor incidents with no lasting injury and undisputed fault can often be handled directly with the insurer. Representation tends to add the most value when injuries are significant, fault is disputed, multiple parties are involved, or an insurer has denied the claim or made a low offer.
