What a personal injury attorney handles

Personal injury attorneys handle claims for people injured through another party's carelessness. The work spans investigation, dealing with insurers, valuing a claim once treatment stabilizes, and filing suit when negotiation does not produce a fair result.

Common personal injury attorney cases in Virginia

  • Collisions involving passenger vehicles, commercial trucks, and rideshare drivers
  • Slip, trip, and fall incidents on business or residential property
  • Injuries caused by inadequate security or unsafe building conditions
  • Product defects that cause injury during ordinary use
  • Claims where an insurer has denied liability or made an inadequate offer

How these claims are proven

Attorneys build these claims from the medical record outward. Consistent treatment, an unbroken timeline between the incident and symptoms, and objective findings from imaging or testing are what make an injury claim durable when an insurer starts looking for reasons to discount it.

Compensation in personal injury attorney claims

Compensation typically covers medical costs, wage loss, future care where needed, and non-economic harm. Where a lasting impairment affects your ability to work, future earning capacity often becomes the largest single component of the claim.

Deadlines that apply

Every state sets a statute of limitations for injury lawsuits, and claims involving a government entity often carry far shorter notice deadlines measured in months. Because those periods differ by state and by claim type, confirm the deadline that applies to your situation with a lawyer licensed in Virginia rather than relying on a general figure. What to expect when you contact a firm

Most personal injury attorneys offer a free initial consultation, and the great majority work on a contingency fee, meaning you owe no attorney fee unless they recover money for you. That first conversation is usually a screening call: they will ask what happened, when it happened, whether you have received medical treatment, and whether you have spoken with any insurer.

If the firm takes the case, the early work typically involves sending a letter of representation so adjusters contact them instead of you, gathering the police or incident report, collecting your medical records and bills, and identifying every insurance policy that might apply. They will generally wait to value the claim until your treatment stabilizes, because settling before then means settling without knowing what your future care will cost.

Before signing anything, ask who will handle your file day to day, what the fee percentage is and when it increases, whether case costs are deducted before or after the fee is calculated, and what happens to those costs if the case is unsuccessful. Get the fee agreement in writing and read it away from the office.

Frequently asked questions

How many personal injury attorneys are listed in Virginia?

Our directory currently lists 26 firms serving Virginia. You can compare them on the map above by rating, review volume, and business type.

How much does it cost to hire a personal injury attorney in Virginia?

Most personal injury firms work on contingency, meaning you pay attorney fees only if they recover money for you, and initial consultations are typically free. Percentages and the treatment of case costs vary between firms, so ask for the fee agreement in writing before signing.

Do I need a lawyer for a personal injury claim?

Not every claim requires representation. Minor incidents with no lasting injury and undisputed fault can often be handled directly with the insurer. Representation tends to add the most value when injuries are significant, fault is disputed, multiple parties are involved, or an insurer has denied the claim or made a low offer.
