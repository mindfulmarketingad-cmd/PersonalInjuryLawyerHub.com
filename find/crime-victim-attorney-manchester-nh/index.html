What a crime victim attorney handles

Crime victim claims are civil actions brought by people harmed by criminal conduct. They are separate from the criminal prosecution: a prosecutor pursues punishment on behalf of the state, while a civil claim seeks compensation for the victim. A civil case can proceed regardless of whether charges were filed, and it uses a lower burden of proof than a criminal trial.

Common crime victim attorney cases in Manchester, New Hampshire

  • Assaults occurring on business or rental property with inadequate security
  • Injuries in parking garages, hotels, or apartment complexes with known safety problems
  • Harm caused by negligent hiring or supervision of an employee
  • Injuries at venues where prior similar incidents were ignored
  • Claims involving state crime victim compensation programs

How these claims are proven

Many of these claims proceed on a negligent security theory: that a property owner knew or should have known of a foreseeable risk and failed to take reasonable precautions such as adequate lighting, functioning locks, or security personnel. Evidence of prior incidents at or near the property is often central. Separately, most states operate a crime victim compensation fund that can help with medical costs and lost wages, and those programs typically impose their own reporting and filing deadlines that are much shorter than civil deadlines.

Compensation in crime victim attorney claims

Civil claims can cover medical and psychological treatment, lost income, and pain and suffering. Because the trauma is frequently ongoing, the cost of counseling and mental health care is often a significant component.

Deadlines that apply

Every state sets a statute of limitations for injury lawsuits, and claims involving a government entity often carry far shorter notice deadlines measured in months. Because those periods differ by state and by claim type, confirm the deadline that applies to your situation with a lawyer licensed in New Hampshire rather than relying on a general figure. What to expect when you contact a firm

Most crime victim attorneys offer a free initial consultation, and the great majority work on a contingency fee, meaning you owe no attorney fee unless they recover money for you. That first conversation is usually a screening call: they will ask what happened, when it happened, whether you have received medical treatment, and whether you have spoken with any insurer.

If the firm takes the case, the early work typically involves sending a letter of representation so adjusters contact them instead of you, gathering the police or incident report, collecting your medical records and bills, and identifying every insurance policy that might apply. They will generally wait to value the claim until your treatment stabilizes, because settling before then means settling without knowing what your future care will cost.

Before signing anything, ask who will handle your file day to day, what the fee percentage is and when it increases, whether case costs are deducted before or after the fee is calculated, and what happens to those costs if the case is unsuccessful. Get the fee agreement in writing and read it away from the office.

How much does it cost to hire a crime victim attorney in Manchester, New Hampshire?

Most personal injury firms work on contingency, meaning you pay attorney fees only if they recover money for you, and initial consultations are typically free. Percentages and the treatment of case costs vary between firms, so ask for the fee agreement in writing before signing.

Do I need a lawyer for a crime victim claim?

Not every claim requires representation. Minor incidents with no lasting injury and undisputed fault can often be handled directly with the insurer. Representation tends to add the most value when injuries are significant, fault is disputed, multiple parties are involved, or an insurer has denied the claim or made a low offer.
