What a drunk driving accident attorney handles

Drunk driving accident claims are civil actions brought by people injured by an impaired driver. As with other crime-related claims, the civil case is independent of any criminal DUI prosecution and can proceed on its own timeline.

Common drunk driving accident attorney cases in West Virginia

  • Collisions caused by drivers impaired by alcohol or drugs
  • Wrong-way and head-on crashes associated with impairment
  • Late-night and early-morning collisions near bars and entertainment districts
  • Claims involving a commercial driver operating while impaired
  • Dram shop claims against establishments that overserved a visibly intoxicated patron

How these claims are proven

A criminal conviction, chemical test results, field sobriety results, and body camera footage can all support the civil claim, though the civil case does not depend on a conviction. Many states also recognize dram shop or social host liability, allowing claims against a bar, restaurant, or in some cases a private host who served a visibly intoxicated person or a minor. Those statutes vary considerably and often carry their own shorter notice requirements.

Compensation in drunk driving accident attorney claims

Alongside standard compensatory damages, impaired driving cases are among the categories where punitive damages may be available, because the conduct is often found to rise above ordinary negligence. Availability and caps on punitive damages differ substantially by state.

Deadlines that apply

Every state sets a statute of limitations for injury lawsuits, and claims involving a government entity often carry far shorter notice deadlines measured in months. Because those periods differ by state and by claim type, confirm the deadline that applies to your situation with a lawyer licensed in West Virginia rather than relying on a general figure. What to expect when you contact a firm

Most drunk driving accident attorneys offer a free initial consultation, and the great majority work on a contingency fee, meaning you owe no attorney fee unless they recover money for you. That first conversation is usually a screening call: they will ask what happened, when it happened, whether you have received medical treatment, and whether you have spoken with any insurer.

If the firm takes the case, the early work typically involves sending a letter of representation so adjusters contact them instead of you, gathering the police or incident report, collecting your medical records and bills, and identifying every insurance policy that might apply. They will generally wait to value the claim until your treatment stabilizes, because settling before then means settling without knowing what your future care will cost.

Before signing anything, ask who will handle your file day to day, what the fee percentage is and when it increases, whether case costs are deducted before or after the fee is calculated, and what happens to those costs if the case is unsuccessful. Get the fee agreement in writing and read it away from the office.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to hire a drunk driving accident attorney in West Virginia?

Most personal injury firms work on contingency, meaning you pay attorney fees only if they recover money for you, and initial consultations are typically free. Percentages and the treatment of case costs vary between firms, so ask for the fee agreement in writing before signing.

Do I need a lawyer for a drunk driving accident claim?

Not every claim requires representation. Minor incidents with no lasting injury and undisputed fault can often be handled directly with the insurer. Representation tends to add the most value when injuries are significant, fault is disputed, multiple parties are involved, or an insurer has denied the claim or made a low offer.
