What a slip and fall lawyer handles

Slip and fall claims fall under premises liability: the principle that property owners and occupiers must keep their premises reasonably safe for people who are lawfully there. These cases are frequently contested, because the property owner will usually argue either that the hazard was obvious or that they had no opportunity to fix it.

Common slip and fall lawyer cases in Rhode Island

  • Wet or recently mopped floors without adequate warning signage
  • Ice and snow accumulation at entrances, walkways, and parking areas
  • Broken, uneven, or poorly lit stairways and handrails
  • Torn carpeting, loose mats, and unmarked changes in floor level
  • Merchandise, cords, or debris left in walkways

How these claims are proven

The pivotal question in most slip and fall cases is notice: did the owner know about the hazard, or should they have known about it through reasonable inspection? Evidence such as maintenance logs, inspection schedules, prior incident reports, and surveillance footage often decides the case. Because video is frequently overwritten within days or weeks, a preservation request sent early can be the difference between proving notice and guessing at it.

Compensation in slip and fall lawyer claims

Fall injuries skew toward fractures, head injuries, and shoulder and hip damage, particularly for older adults. Claims commonly include emergency and follow-up care, surgery and rehabilitation, lost income, and compensation for lasting mobility limitations.

Deadlines that apply

Every state sets a statute of limitations for injury lawsuits, and claims involving a government entity often carry far shorter notice deadlines measured in months. Because those periods differ by state and by claim type, confirm the deadline that applies to your situation with a lawyer licensed in Rhode Island rather than relying on a general figure. What to expect when you contact a firm

Most slip and fall lawyers offer a free initial consultation, and the great majority work on a contingency fee, meaning you owe no attorney fee unless they recover money for you. That first conversation is usually a screening call: they will ask what happened, when it happened, whether you have received medical treatment, and whether you have spoken with any insurer.

If the firm takes the case, the early work typically involves sending a letter of representation so adjusters contact them instead of you, gathering the police or incident report, collecting your medical records and bills, and identifying every insurance policy that might apply. They will generally wait to value the claim until your treatment stabilizes, because settling before then means settling without knowing what your future care will cost.

Before signing anything, ask who will handle your file day to day, what the fee percentage is and when it increases, whether case costs are deducted before or after the fee is calculated, and what happens to those costs if the case is unsuccessful. Get the fee agreement in writing and read it away from the office.

Frequently asked questions

How many slip and fall lawyers are listed in Rhode Island?

Our directory currently lists 22 firms serving Rhode Island. You can compare them on the map above by rating, review volume, and business type.

How much does it cost to hire a slip and fall lawyer in Rhode Island?

Most personal injury firms work on contingency, meaning you pay attorney fees only if they recover money for you, and initial consultations are typically free. Percentages and the treatment of case costs vary between firms, so ask for the fee agreement in writing before signing.

Do I need a lawyer for a slip and fall claim?

Not every claim requires representation. Minor incidents with no lasting injury and undisputed fault can often be handled directly with the insurer. Representation tends to add the most value when injuries are significant, fault is disputed, multiple parties are involved, or an insurer has denied the claim or made a low offer.
