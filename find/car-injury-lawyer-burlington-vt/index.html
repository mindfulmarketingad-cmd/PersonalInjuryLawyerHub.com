What a car injury lawyer handles

Car injury claims arise from collisions between passenger vehicles. They are the most common type of personal injury claim, which also means insurers handle them at volume and have well-developed playbooks for minimizing them.

Common car injury lawyer cases in Burlington, Vermont

  • Rear-end collisions, which frequently produce neck and back injuries
  • Intersection and left-turn crashes where fault is disputed
  • Sideswipe and lane-change collisions
  • Multi-vehicle chain-reaction crashes
  • Hit-and-run and uninsured or underinsured motorist claims

How these claims are proven

Liability often comes down to the police report, physical damage patterns, traffic camera or dashcam footage, and witness accounts. Damages come down to your medical records. The most common insurer arguments are that the impact was too minor to cause injury, that a gap in treatment shows you recovered, or that a degenerative finding on imaging predates the crash.

Compensation in car injury lawyer claims

Claims typically include emergency treatment, imaging, physical therapy, pain management, vehicle-related losses, lost wages, and pain and suffering. Where symptoms persist past maximum medical improvement, future care and permanent impairment become part of the valuation.

Deadlines that apply

Every state sets a statute of limitations for injury lawsuits, and claims involving a government entity often carry far shorter notice deadlines measured in months. Because those periods differ by state and by claim type, confirm the deadline that applies to your situation with a lawyer licensed in Vermont rather than relying on a general figure. What to expect when you contact a firm

Most car injury lawyers offer a free initial consultation, and the great majority work on a contingency fee, meaning you owe no attorney fee unless they recover money for you. That first conversation is usually a screening call: they will ask what happened, when it happened, whether you have received medical treatment, and whether you have spoken with any insurer.

If the firm takes the case, the early work typically involves sending a letter of representation so adjusters contact them instead of you, gathering the police or incident report, collecting your medical records and bills, and identifying every insurance policy that might apply. They will generally wait to value the claim until your treatment stabilizes, because settling before then means settling without knowing what your future care will cost.

Before signing anything, ask who will handle your file day to day, what the fee percentage is and when it increases, whether case costs are deducted before or after the fee is calculated, and what happens to those costs if the case is unsuccessful. Get the fee agreement in writing and read it away from the office.

Frequently asked questions

How many car injury lawyers are listed in Burlington, Vermont?

Our directory currently lists 6 firms serving Burlington, Vermont. You can compare them on the map above by rating, review volume, and business type.

How much does it cost to hire a car injury lawyer in Burlington, Vermont?

Most personal injury firms work on contingency, meaning you pay attorney fees only if they recover money for you, and initial consultations are typically free. Percentages and the treatment of case costs vary between firms, so ask for the fee agreement in writing before signing.

Do I need a lawyer for a car injury claim?

Not every claim requires representation. Minor incidents with no lasting injury and undisputed fault can often be handled directly with the insurer. Representation tends to add the most value when injuries are significant, fault is disputed, multiple parties are involved, or an insurer has denied the claim or made a low offer.
