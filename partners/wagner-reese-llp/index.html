Preparing to contact Wagner Reese, LLP

Bring as much of the following as you have. Firms can work without it, but a first consultation is far more productive when the basics are in front of them:

  • The police or incident report, or the report number
  • Photographs of the scene, vehicles, hazard, or injuries
  • Names and contact details for any witnesses
  • Medical records, discharge paperwork, and bills received so far
  • Your insurance policy, and any correspondence from either insurer
  • Documentation of missed work and lost income
  • A written timeline of what happened while your memory is fresh

Questions worth asking on that first call include who will handle your file day to day, what portion of the firm's practice is personal injury, what the contingency percentage is and when it increases, whether case costs are deducted before or after the fee is calculated, and what happens to those costs if the case is unsuccessful. Our guide to choosing a personal injury lawyer covers all ten questions in detail, and what a personal injury lawyer costs explains the fee structure.

Before signing anything, confirm the firm's attorneys are licensed and in good standing through your state bar association's public lookup. It takes about two minutes and is the single most useful check available to you.
